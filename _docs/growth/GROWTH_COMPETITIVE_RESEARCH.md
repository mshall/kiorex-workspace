## 2. What the incumbents actually do (observed)

### 2.1 Zocdoc (US)
- Revenue model is almost exactly the Kiorex model: **pay per new-patient booking, no subscription** (S1). Key differences worth noting: Zocdoc charges at booking regardless of attendance (waiver only if the patient cancels within 24 h), while Kiorex charges at attended new patient (KX-12, ADR-007), which is a stronger provider promise and a stronger fraud shield on the provider side but weaker cash predictability for Kiorex.
- It protects the provider from paying for patients they already had: existing patients never trigger a fee and a practice can claim a credit within 7 days (S1). Kiorex's 24-month lookback plus dispute window is a similar but stricter instrument.
- Provider controls matter: **monthly spend cap**, pause, per-provider switch (S1). Kiorex's Lite monthly cap and "higher of S or C" already cover part; a visible per-month cap in the Growth workspace is cheap and builds trust.
- Provider-owned channels: bookings through the practice's own website are fee-free for existing patients and some partner paths (S2). That is the same logic as `CLINIC_LINK`.
- Self-reported lifts (78% from Google Business Profile, 8x ROI) (S2) show Google Business Profile booking is the lever Zocdoc pushes first. KX-14 already lists Google Maps/Business booking links.

### 2.2 Vezeeta (Egypt, the main local comparator)
Observed on the Cairo listing page (S4):
- URL families: city listing `/en/doctor/all-specialities/cairo`, specialty pages `/en/doctor/{specialty}/egypt`, doctor profile `/en/dr/doctor-{name}-{specialty}`, clinic pages `/en/clinic/{name}`; filters in query parameters (`gender`, `availability`, `promo`, `fees`, `payment`, `doctorEntity`, `page`); pagination with a very large page count (865 pages for 8,645 doctors).
- Page content: H1 "Best Doctors in Cairo", title and result count, five FAQ blocks, a health-articles section, per-doctor fee in EGP, estimated waiting time, rating count or "Joined Recently", trait badges, labels "Sponsored", "New", "Accept Online Payment", Shamel discounted price.
- Structured data: BreadcrumbList and Physician/Dentist entities with price range and area served (S4). **Observation: the breadcrumb points to the national page while the page URL is under `cairo`, and 865 paginated pages exist; both are weaknesses a newcomer can avoid** (consistent canonical, capped pagination, thin-page guard).
- Patient proposition: "fees shown are the doctor's actual fees with no extra charges" and instant auto-confirmed booking (S5). Kiorex's differentiators from KX-14: never pay for no-shows, verified-visit reviews, real Kiorly slots.
- Commission: patients free, providers pay a fee varying by specialty and location, plus subscription scheduling (S6, C grade, old).
- Insurance and "promo" filters imply a heavy price-led marketplace; sponsored placement exists. For Kiorex, sponsored listings stay a post-liquidity item (KX-03 P3, flag `kiorex.sponsored_listings`).

### 2.3 Doctolib (France, Germany)
Evidence is thin (S8, S9). What is supportable: patients book from the practitioner's calendar on Doctolib; reminders and waiting-list alerts exist; Doctolib's own decks tell practitioners to put **several booking buttons on their own website** and to complete profiles. No public widget spec or URL scheme was found. Lesson, treated as a hypothesis not a fact: provider adoption comes from (a) booking buttons placed on the doctor's own site and profile (same as widgets and links) and (b) calendar reliability (no double booking), which Kiorex already guarantees through Kiorly as system of record.

### 2.4 Practo (India)
Evidence is C grade (S10). Supportable: free profile for doctors, city and specialty listing URLs, vendor-reported `Physician` JSON-LD, subscription practice software (Ray) as a second revenue engine, acquisitions of software companies to get real-time availability. This mirrors the Kiorly plus Kiorex pairing. No claims here beyond that.

## 4. Where Kiorex should deliberately differ

1. **Charge on attendance, not at booking** (stronger provider promise than Zocdoc and likely than Vezeeta; Vezeeta's exact terms were not found). Keep it.
2. **Evidence-first commission**: publish the rule, show the evidence, auto-resolve disputes. No competitor evidence was found for this; it is a trust differentiator, not a proven one.
3. **No health-condition targeting and no patient identifiers to ad platforms**: a brand promise as well as compliance. Competitors' practices here were not researched.
4. **One system of record for appointments**: avoids the double-booking and "phone me back" failures reported about marketplaces (S9, C grade).
5. **Arabic-first, WhatsApp-first** entry paths.

## 5. Risks in the competitive picture

- Vezeeta has years of Arabic search history, a very large page inventory and a patient subscription (S4, S7). A newcomer will not win head terms quickly; the plan targets long-tail specialty plus district pages where Kiorex has real live slots, which is also what the thin-page guard requires.
- Large listing inventories invite thin pages. The Google policies (S11) are explicit that near-duplicate city and region pages routing to one destination are doorway abuse. The guard in the Architecture doc is therefore a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have.
- WhatsApp costs and policy are moving (S14: service-message charges start 1 Oct 2026, categories differ). Model cost per booking with the current Meta table before committing to proactive messaging.
- Legal reading of Egyptian direct-marketing rules is secondary-source only (S15). Do not send marketing in production until counsel signs.
